Den Gear Bench

Dingo gear, one piece at a time, made from the Smithy's own materials and drawn straight onto the hero's rig so you see it move. He keeps every piece he's been given on, in layer order (armor under, collar and jewellery over), so each new piece is judged against the ones already there. Every piece is a short code like a weapon.

Notes

  • One rule for every piece: the band is the material of the whole piece, the fitting is its accent (buckle, studs, trim, rivets, edges). Pick Oak and Antler on every tab and everything he wears is oak with antler hardware. Oak and Dark wood are also offered as fittings, so a piece can be one self-coloured material.
  • Same forge, same materials, same brush. The band is a Smithy wrap, the fitting a Smithy fitting, the gem the Smithy's diamond with its rim and glint, and every shape is drawn the Smithy's way: outline, base, shade on the lower half, a light strip on the upper edge. The name is built the Smithy way too. No new palettes.
  • Collar: plain, studded, tag, rope. The gem is always centred on the band, studs spaced evenly either side; the buckle, tag or knot sits at the throat.
  • Tail guard: sleeve or guard, both in the band material with the fitting as the accent (trim on the sleeve; a metal edge and a rivet on each of the guard's four plates). An Oak guard matches an Oak collar. The guard's three gaps are the ring slots; the gem takes the middle one.
  • Tail rings: three slots in the guard's gaps (the guard's own spacing decides where they sit), each ring its own piece. The ring itself is the fitting material (a metal, antler or one of the two woods); the band material is the gem's socket, a slightly larger diamond behind the gem, shown as an empty setting when there is no gem. Each ring has its own gem, rarity and infusion. "Sync the three rings" (on by default) keeps material, setting, rarity and infusion matched while each ring keeps its own gem; turn it off to change one ring alone.
  • Bracelets (cuffs and bracelets): four slots, two on each front leg, drawn over the sleeve and sock and riding the forearm. One slot type, three styles you can mix in any slot: the cuff is wide with one centred gem (more protection); the bracelet is slim with three small gems banded together; the twin bracelet has two. Each gem sits in its own socket in the band material, the band itself in the fitting material (as on the rings). The two slots on a leg are spaced for the widest case, two cuffs, so a slimmer piece centres in its slot and swapping one for another never moves its neighbour. The two far-leg slots show when he walks.
  • Helmet (the hood): a fitted hood cut from each dog's own head, from under the collar to a line across the muzzle (the muzzle and lip stay bare). The eye shows through a rimmed opening and the ears rise out of rimmed openings and move freely, laid back too (the dogs' ears are drawn behind the head, so the hood goes over the head and leaves them free). The hood is the band material, the rims and front trim the fitting, and the gem sits where the hood is widest, measured rather than placed by eye. On the companion the hood stops behind the eye, with no eye rim or front trim, because its eye and snout sit differently.
  • Roll random outfits: gives each dog its own random gear in every slot (random material, fitting, style, gem, rarity and infusion, with some slots left empty), listed with their codes, so you can see how pieces combine. Roll again for another; picking anything on a tab, or Back to the bench, returns to the bench's own pieces.
  • Body armor (the torso piece): one shape cut from each dog's own outline, so it is flush on the back and chest. It starts under the collar, ends at a rear edge in front of the tail, and its hem follows the belly with hide tufts. It is drawn over the legs and under the collar, and follows the body as it bobs. It comes with five sleeves, one on each leg and one on the tail (under the tail guard and rings): moving pieces on the thigh and the upper arm (and the forearm where the elbow needs it), each cut from that dog's own leg, ending square to the bone with a tufted, stitched hem. Hind sleeves end just above the knee, front sleeves just above the wrist, and the tail sleeve runs from the root to just short of the tip. Only the Hide style so far; Scale and Plated come once Hide is right.
  • Layer order: paw and leg covers under the armor's sleeves, bracelets over the sleeves, under the torso piece, with the collar over the torso piece. On the tail, the guard goes under the rings.
  • Match across pieces: with it on, the band, fitting, gem, rarity and infusion you pick on one tab are used for every piece he wears, so you can judge a whole set in one material; style stays per piece.
  • Levels, stats and set bonuses are not here yet, by design: this is the drawing and the code.
