Frames where a filled point looks unsure: a weak match, a jump, or a bone that changed length. Click one, drag the point to the right spot (it becomes a key point) and the joint refills around it.

How it fills the frames between your points

Each filled point is found by matching the look of the spot at your nearest key points (never the frame before, so it cannot creep onto loose fur), searched around the path between your keys, then the smoothest path through the best few matches is kept, with each bone held near its length. Points it is unsure of are drawn hollow and listed under Check. Ideas from Tracker (Open Source Physics), Kinovea, DLTdv, SLEAP and Anipose; the code is our own.

Where to click (bony points, not fur)

Modes

Manual (the default): only the points you place exist. Press Fill gaps now when you want the frames between filled from your points, and turn Filled points off again to hide them. Assisted: the frames between fill as you go. Auto adjust my points nudges your clicks up to 3 px to match the same spot on your nearby frames; a dotted tick shows where you clicked, and Revert adjustments (or right-click a point) puts them back.

Tracks

Pick a track above the video (or press 1–5) to work on one leg, or the head and back, at a time: clicking, the next joint, the next frame, Fill, Auto adjust, copy, paste and Clear all stay inside it, and the other joints are dimmed (or hidden, or shown). + New track makes your own, from any joints or a new point such as the nose. Right-click one of your tracks twice to delete it; its points stay.

How exact is hand placing?

Studies of people clicking joints on video report about 2–3 pixels between tries and 2–3° on a joint angle, more near the knee where skin slides over bone. Placing the same frames twice and comparing is a good test of your own spread.
