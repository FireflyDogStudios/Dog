# Does it read as a dog? The canine review checklist

From GrumpyDingo's research notes (Oct 8, 2026): what animators, wildlife artists and game studios keep checking, roughly in order of how much it matters. Use it on every canine (wolf first, then the Husky, dingo, coyote, jackal, wild dog), by eye, in the Wolf Review Bench ("Reads as a dog?" tab, answers saved to its shared store). Where `./den dogcheck` can measure a question, the bench shows its result beside it.

**Note on the sources.** The research arrived with links (Envato Tuts+, articles on Aaron Blaise's and Monika Zagrobelna's methods, studio write-ups on Bolt, Togo and theHunter). Several of the linked pages sit on unrelated domains that look like content farms, so treat the links as unverified. The points themselves match standard canine anatomy and our own `den-dog-anatomy` skill, which is why they are used here.

## For a flat 2D game, silhouette comes first
Muzzle length, ear shape, the hock bend and the tail carriage carry almost all of the recognition. The rules below are what keep that silhouette honest.

## 1. Silhouette
- In black silhouette, at game size, does it read as a canine at once? *(dogcheck: pieces)*
- Muzzle length: not so short that it reads as a dog or husky, not so thin and pointed that it reads as a fox? *(dogcheck: fit)*
- Ears: triangular, upright and on top of the skull; not oversized (fox) or round (hyena)?
- Hock bend: does the hind leg zig-zag in the outline (knee forward, hock back)? *(dogcheck: joints)*
- Tail: set as the end of the spine, hanging as a full brush (wolf); not dainty or stubby?

## 2. Head (where most canine drawings succeed or fail)
- A stop: the forehead drops between the eyes, rather than a flat ramp from the nose to the skull.
- Parallel planes: the top of the muzzle and the top of the skull run close to parallel. A cat's slope sharply.
- Two masses: a round cranium and a wedge-shaped muzzle, about one to one for a wolf. This ratio is what sets the breed.
- Eyes: on the cranium's middle line and set slightly to the side. Almond to round, amber or yellow for a wolf (never blue), with a dark rim and no large whites.
- Nose: a simple anchor shape that isn't over-rendered.

## 3. Legs (digitigrade, with the right bends)
- It stands on its toes, with the heel (hock) held well off the ground. *(dogcheck: feet)*
- The real knee (stifle) sits high and close to the body, and bends forward. *(dogcheck: joints)*
- The hock bends backward and sits just behind the point of the buttock. *(dogcheck: joints)*
- The front "knee" is a wrist: the elbow sits at the brisket and the wrist bends backward. *(dogcheck: joints)*
- Paws are compact and blunt, with no hooked claws (those read as cat).
- Legs taper from muscled tops (thigh, upper arm) to slim lower legs; they aren't tubes. *(dogcheck: attach)*

## 4. Body
- A deep, narrow chest and a modest tuck-up; not a box, not a sausage. *(dogcheck: fit)*
- The shoulder floats: dogs have no collarbone, so the blade slides. A fixed pin reads as a plastic toy.
- A wolf's shoulder hump (withers), with a level back. *(dogcheck: fit)*
- Fur reads as mass (ruff, brush tail) rather than texture: a rug draped over a statue.

## 5. Motion (half of reading as a dog)
- Planted paws stay put while the body passes over them. *(dogcheck: feet)*
- A walk reads as two bipeds slightly offset, with clear contact, passing, down and up poses.
- The spine flexes and the body rides over the legs, rather than a rigid box on legs. In a run the spine bends like a bow.
- Follow-through: the head settles a little late and the tail trails.
- Idle: standing still, it looks alive (breathing, blinking, ear flicks, looking around). Avalanche credits theHunter's companion dog to exactly this.

## 6. Not-a-dog tells (any yes is a problem)
- **Cat:** a round short skull, hooked claws, a hyper-flexible spine, prominent whiskers.
- **Fox:** a thin pointed muzzle, oversized ears, slim legs, a dainty tail.
- **Bear:** flat feet (plantigrade), a stub tail, heavy forearms.
- **Hyena:** a sloped back, short hind legs, round ears.

## Studio practice worth keeping
- **Use the wolf as the baseline.** Domestic dog anatomy is a variation on it, so build a credible wolf first, then adjust proportions per breed. That is our order: hero3 first, the Husky as a preset.
- **Real reference over diagrams.** Riot drew on African wild dogs for Naafiri; their ear signals read as canine.
- **A functional skeleton under any fantasy.** Brynn Metheney rebuilt the Terror Dog so it could actually run and carry its weight.
- **Body language as a system.** For Bolt, Disney rigged neck fur that rises when excited and flattens when running, which maps to our hackles tell.
