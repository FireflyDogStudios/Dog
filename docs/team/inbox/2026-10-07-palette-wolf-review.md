# Wolf muscle body, round 3: what reads wrong as a form (art style lane)
From: Palette · 2026-10-07
Needs from Firefly: nothing (FYI). One suggestion for the review renders, in the last bullet.
Looked at `before-after.png` and `after-muscle-map.png`. These are not repeats of the known faults.

**At a glance**
- **Rhythm: the body is one tube of even depth.** A canine trunk reads big, small, big (deep chest, waist, haunch). Here the depth barely changes from shoulder to hip, so the belly tuck alone won't fix it. The haunch also needs to read as a mass.
- **Weight is all at the front.** The shoulder block is the biggest, most built-up shape. The hindquarter is two small floating patches (the red and lime gluteals) with no thigh under them: from the hip down to the stifle, the hind leg comes out of the body as bare bone. A wolf's drive is a long, deep thigh reaching down to the stifle. Until that's there, the animal reads front-heavy and weak behind, more hyena than wolf.
- **Gesture: a stiff stretched stance.** The forearm slants forward to the foot, and the hind foot sits behind the point of the buttock. That's a show stack, like a rocking horse. A neutral standing wolf has its forelegs near vertical under the withers and a narrower base.
- **The topline has three bumps.** The withers rise, the back dips, then the loin bumps (the known fault). The neck then climbs in a nearly straight ramp to the skull. There's no single line of action from nose to tail. A relaxed wolf is one low, gentle curve, with its head carried near the line of its back.
- **The head is joined like a collar.** There's no throat line under the neck, and the neck meets the skull at a hard vertical edge. Even before the head is rebuilt, the underside of the neck should flow into the jaw.
- **Tail set:** the tail leaves the top of the sacrum and carries on the topline. Wolves set it lower and let it hang. Worth fixing when the tail is redone, because the angle of the tail is half the dog's mood later.

**For the skin and fur steps (so it can carry a strong game style)**
- **Judge silhouettes, not shading.** Add two renders to each review next to the shaded one: a solid black silhouette, and the same at game size (about 120 px tall). The game is flat two-tone, so if the silhouette is right the style will follow.
- **Three big masses plus the head:** chest and shoulder, ribs and waist, haunch. Skin should bridge the muscle seams into those few shapes. The stair-stepped belly and rump edges and the patch outlines must not survive into skin.
- **Fur is silhouette, not texture.** Model it as offsets on its own layer: neck ruff, withers, chest, britches on the back of the thigh, the brush tail. Keeping it separate lets the style push it later without touching the measured body.
- **Keep the measured base and the style apart.** Style should come from a few parameters (head size, leg thickness, chest depth, fur volume), not hand edits to the mesh. The real wolf stays true, and the game wolf is a dialled version of it.
- **Plan for small size now.** At game scale the measured lower legs will be 2-3 px wide. The style version will need thicker lower legs and a readable paw shape, so leave room for that in the rig.
