# Licence check: dog generators and rig tools (Atlas, Oct 8, 2026)

Asked by Firefly for GrumpyDingo; shortlist from Scout (`ref/research/scout/19-dog-generators/`). Infinigen (BSD-3) skipped, as agreed. **This is what the texts say, not legal advice.** Nothing was bought and nobody was contacted; no accounts were made.

The studio's use: a 2D desktop game, sold commercially, drawn as flat vector art. Candidate 3D sources would be rendered and restyled; possibly pre-rendered sprites would ship.

## Bottom line
- **Daz Dog 8 + Phenotypes: OK with cost for 2D art.** The standard terms cover 2D renders and sprites. Shipping 3D data needs a separate Interactive License.
- **Fab pack: not determined.** `fab.com` blocks every fetch (HTTP 403). A person must read the EULA and the listing.
- **Tripo: free plan not OK, paid plan OK with cost.** **Meshy: free plan not OK as it stands, paid plan OK with cost.** Both vendors' documents contradict themselves on free-plan ownership.
- **Spine: OK with cost** (Professional, one named person per licence, $500,000 revenue threshold). **Rive: OK with cost** ($9 per seat per month), but weaker fit for Pixi.

## Details that matter for a decision
- **Daz, in-game sale clause.** Addendum 3.0 (which applies only when you hold an Interactive License or Game Developer License) says that within a game "the sale or purchase of the CRT Content... or either two-dimensional or three-dimensional derivatives thereof as a separately-purchased commodity or upgrade using items of actual or virtual worth is prohibited without prior written consent". Whether that touches pure 2D sprites under the standard agreement is **not settled by the text I saw**. It matters if the game sells cosmetic dogs or gear for real or virtual currency.
- **Daz, "redraw as vector art".** The EULA text covers 2D "renderings" and "two-dimensional images... derived by User from the Content". It does not say "sprite" (that word is on the Interactive License info page, a summary). Redrawing in vector style is not addressed explicitly. A written answer from Daz would settle it; that is contact, so it is GrumpyDingo's.
- **Daz, AI.** The EULA excludes using the content "in connection with... any AI engine" that can auto-generate derivative material. Feeding Daz renders into AI image tools is out.
- **Daz, per person.** Every team member or contractor who opens the Daz assets needs their own licence.
- **Spine, revenue threshold.** Licence §1.1.1: Essential and Professional apply only while "gross revenue, investment income, venture capital, or other financing" is under $500,000 in the aggregate over the last 12 months; above it, Enterprise is required. Financing counts, not just sales. The purchase page words it two ways ("more than" and "or more").
- **Tripo and Meshy, free tier.** Do not ship free-tier outputs. If the studio ever uses Meshy's free plan, §3.2 would require credit to Meshy, and the contradictions above would still stand.
- **Tripo quadruped auto-rig.** The API docs list quadruped rigs ("v2.5-20260210, non-humanoid creatures", 25 credits); the terms treat rigs as Outputs. Whether the free plan includes rigging is not stated.
