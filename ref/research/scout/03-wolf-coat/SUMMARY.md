**Measured fur depth (skin to fur surface), winter pelts, thickest areas:** wolf (grouped with grizzly bear, not separable) **54-65 mm**; Eskimo dog / red fox / beaver 31-47 mm; arctic fox 45-56 mm (Scholander et al. 1950, Fig. 3, digitised; B). This is the one true "outline standoff" number. Compared with back guard hairs of 100-145 mm, **depth ≈ 0.5 × guard-hair length.**

**Where the long hair is** (H&N p.126): longest on the front of the back and on the neck; a patch of especially long hair on the shoulder; a crest on the upper neck; cheek tufts; legs short and close-lying from the elbow and just above the hock down; ears short-haired and standing clear of the fur; forehead short. Summer fur is "much more sparse and shorter" and coarse. Tail hair is much shorter at the root than along the rest.

## Suggested fur offset (outline standoff from skin), side view
Wolf withers height (WH) 750 mm. **Only the withers and back winter values are anchored by a measurement (Scholander depth 54-65 mm); everything else is EST** (≈0.5 × the guard-hair lengths above; summer ≈0.5 × winter because the underfur is shed and guard hairs are about 25 % shorter).

## Gaps
- No measured depth for any region except the "thickly furred areas"; chest, throat, thigh and belly are estimates.
- No summer fur depth at all; summer hair lengths only for the back.
- Mech 1974 (Mammalian Species 37; public domain, US government work) is blocked by Cloudflare: download by hand from https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/usgsnpwrc/334 . Young & Goldman 1944 is borrow-only; the originals of Kennedy 1982 and Adorjan & Kolenosky 1969 were not read.
