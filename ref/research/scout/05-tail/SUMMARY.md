The model has 18-19 caudal vertebrae (dog average 20, wolf about 20). Cd1 is 14 mm long; lengths rise to 27 mm at Cd7-Cd9 (0.069 of tail length) and fall to about 17 mm at Cd17. Width at the base with the transverse processes is 28 mm (Cd1-2).

So the bone is about 5-10 % of the brush width in the middle of the tail; the rest is hair. That agrees with the pelt hair lengths: tail guard hair and underfur 70-75 mm, 95-100 mm at the tip (Alaska State Museum, C), lying at an angle on both sides. H&N: tail hairs are much shorter at the root. The brush is widest at about 55-70 % of the tail length.

## Relaxed carriage
- Wolf: about **-80°** to the topline in the PD photo (the tail hangs slightly forward of vertical against the thighs). H&N: it "hangs directly downward", "as if broken at the base", reaching the hock, and is raised only to back level in a fast gallop. This supports `wolf.yaml` -75° (range -83 to -54).
- Dingo: about **-55°** in one photo (body slightly turned; EST).
- Carolina Dog (UKC): the tail is set as a continuation of the spine with a slight lift at the base. Alert, it is held about 45° above the horizontal with a fish-hook end (the last third bends back toward the head); when gaiting it may be carried low.
- Golden jackal: the tail always droops, reaching the heel (H&N).

## Gaps
- No published brush-width profile for any canid; two photos with one animal each, and the tail base is hidden in both.
- No open wolf or dingo caudal vertebra measurements; the values are scaled from one domestic-dog mesh.
- More photos of a tail hanging clear of the legs (side view, from behind or trotting) would firm up the base and mid widths.
