# 04 dingo coat: summary

**Status: not found as numbers; descriptions only. The offset table is an estimate (EST).** Date 2026-10-07. See `NOTE.md` and `data.csv`.

## What exists
- Dingo: "body fur short; tail quite bushy" (Australian Museum). Alpine dingoes have a thicker double coat with a dense undercoat; desert dingoes a shorter, sparser coat that lies close but may stand away on the tail (breed-club page, C).
- Carolina Dog (UKC standard 2023, C): the coat is close-lying; hair on the head, ears and front legs is short and smooth; **coarse, longer guard hairs run over the neck, withers and back**, stand erect when the dog is aroused and can form a darker ridge; the winter coat is "distinctively heavier"; the tail has a moderate brush, heavier on the underside.
- Scale: a "short coat" dog has average body hair under 20 mm (ruler-measured clinical definition, PMC12491747, CC BY 4.0, B).
- Upper references: Eskimo dog / red fox winter fur depth 31-47 mm (Scholander 1950); golden jackal longest dorsal guard hair 66.6 mm (Arpacik 2021).
- No Brisbin paper with coat numbers was found.

Compared with the wolf, the dingo's body fur offset is roughly a quarter in winter (withers 15 vs 60 mm), but its tail brush is about as wide relative to body size. The bushy tail is the dingo's main fur silhouette feature.
