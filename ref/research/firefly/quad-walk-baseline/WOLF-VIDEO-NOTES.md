# Two wolf clips: what they show (Firefly, Oct 8, 2026)

GrumpyDingo's two reference clips are in his Drive (`Wolf.mp4` and `Wolf2.mp4`, 480×270, 15 fps; licence unknown, so they are not stored here, only these observations).
- **Clip 1:** a black wolf in a slow walk, side view, the camera tracking it, 44 frames.
- **Clip 2:** a grey wolf in a travelling trot, three-quarter view, 10 frames.

## What the wolves do
1. **The head rides low.**
   - In clip 1 the nose is down near elbow height, the neck slopes forward and down from the shoulders, and the withers are the highest point of the dog.
   - In clip 2 (travelling) the head sits at or a little below the back line, never above it.
   - The standing photos, and hero3, hold the head higher: that is a standing pose, not a moving one.
2. **Long, reaching steps.**
   - The front paw lands far forward, almost under the nose, with the leg slanted well ahead of the shoulder at touchdown. The body then travels over it.
   - In clip 1 the planted front paw stays down for about 12 of an 18-frame cycle (a duty of about 0.67, in line with the measured 0.59-0.68).
3. **The front paw swings low.**
   - At lift-off the wrist folds tight up under the chest.
   - The forearm then swings forward and the paw unfolds straight out, skimming close to the ground, before it plants.
   - There's no high, prancing lift.
4. **The hind leg pushes long behind.** At push-off the hind paw is well back of the buttock and the leg nearly straight from hip to paw.
5. **Tail:**
   - In the trot (clip 2) it is held out straight behind, about level with or a little below the back.
   - In the slow walk it hangs lower.
   - Either way it isn't a stiff vertical hang.
6. **Front legs are columns in stance:** nearly straight from elbow to paw, with the wrist over-extended a little under load.

## Against hero3 (build 2bd489e)
- **Stride length:**
  - hero3 covers **12.6 units per stride, about 0.5 of its withers height**. Each planted paw moves only 7.6-7.8 units.
  - Measured dog walks: Fischer 2018 Malinois at 1.3 m/s and Catavitello's retrievers at 1.45 strides/s give roughly 0.9 m per stride, about **1.2-1.5 withers heights**.
  - **hero3 takes about half-length steps. That is the shuffle.**
- **Head:** carried above the back. It needs its own joint, so the head (and neck) can drop and lead while walking.
- **Front reach:** hero3's front paw lands only 0.8 units ahead of where it stands. The wolves land it well forward.
- **Tail:** hero3's hangs nearly vertical while walking. It should lift out behind as speed rises.
